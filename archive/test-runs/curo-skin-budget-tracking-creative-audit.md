## 1.4 The Core Problem: Three Platforms Below Minimum

The 3-platform strategy at $2,000/month fails on every structural metric:

**Why this matters operationally:**

1. **Algorithmic learning is additive, not divisible.** Each platform requires a minimum conversion signal to exit learning phase. Splitting budget three ways does not give you "one-third of the benefit" from each platform — it gives you zero benefit from all three, because none can reach the conversion threshold needed to train their algorithms.

2. **Attribution overlap multiplies the problem.** A single customer might see a Meta ad, click a Google Shopping ad, and convert via a TikTok retargeting ad. All three platforms claim the conversion. At $2,000/month, the true conversion volume is low enough that this overlap represents a material distortion of performance data across all platforms.

3. **Creative production cost relative to spend.** TikTok requires platform-native video creative (9:16, UGC-style). Producing 4–6 TikTok creatives per month at a professional standard costs £500–£1,500. At $300/month TikTok spend, creative production cost exceeds or equals media spend.

4. **Frequency and reach conflict.** At $300/month on TikTok, you are reaching a very small audience very infrequently. This is insufficient to build brand recognition or retargeting pools of meaningful size.

## 1.5 The 70/20/10 Rule Assessment

The 70/20/10 rule states: 70% of budget to proven, performing channels; 20% to channels being actively scaled; 10% to testing new channels.

| Bucket | Rule | Required Budget | At $2,000/mo | Assessment |
|--------|------|----------------|--------------|------------|
| **70% — Proven Channel** | ~$1,400/mo | $1,400 to single proven platform | $1,400 is below Meta's optimal operating level | FAIL |
| **20% — Scaling Channel** | ~$400/mo | $400 to scaling platform | Google PMax needs $1,000 minimum — $400 is non-functional | FAIL |
| **10% — Testing Channel** | ~$200/mo | $200 to test channel | TikTok minimum is $1,500/mo — $200 does nothing | FAIL |

The 70/20/10 framework assumes a minimum total budget sufficient for all three buckets to operate above viable thresholds. At $2,000/month, this framework cannot be applied. The business is not yet at the budget level where multi-platform 70/20/10 thinking is relevant.

## 1.7 MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio) Targets

MER = Total Revenue / Total Ad Spend. Unlike ROAS, MER captures blended performance across all channels including view-through and assisted conversions, making it the most accurate efficiency metric for DTC brands running multi-platform.

### Target MER for Curo Skin

| Business Stage | MER Target | Context |
|---------------|------------|---------|
| **Learning phase (Month 1–3)** | 2.0–2.5x | Prioritise data collection over profitability |
| **Optimisation phase (Month 4–6)** | 2.5–3.5x | Improving creative, bidding, audience signals |
| **Scaling phase (Month 7+)** | 3.5–5.0x+ | Scaling proven campaigns on proven platforms |
| **Subscription revenue adjustment** | Add 0.5–1.0x to all targets | Quarterly filter subscriptions raise LTV, justifying lower initial MER |

**Subscription LTV uplift:** A customer buying the shower head at £80 and subscribing to quarterly filters at ~£20–£25/delivery generates £100–£180 in year-one LTV. This means the brand can afford a higher acquisition CPA than a one-time purchase DTC brand. A first-purchase CPA of £40–£60 is defensible if subscription attach rate is ≥30%.

**Current estimated MER at $2,000/month (3 platforms):** Likely 1.0–1.8x. Spread too thin across platforms, all in learning phase, no channel has sufficient data to optimise. This range is below breakeven for most DTC businesses after COGS and fulfilment.

## 1.8 Bidding Strategy Recommendations by Platform

### Meta Ads

| Campaign Type | Recommended Bid Strategy | Notes |
|--------------|--------------------------|-------|
| Advantage+ Shopping (ASC) | Lowest Cost (no cap) | Default — let Meta optimise for maximum purchase volume within budget |
| Prospecting (interest/broad) | Lowest Cost | At low budget, avoid cost caps which restrict delivery |
| Retargeting (once pool is large enough) | Cost Cap at 1.2x target CPA | Add control once retargeting pool exceeds 1,000 users |

**Do NOT use:** Manual bids, Bid Cap at launch. These require sufficient conversion history to set accurately. Premature bid caps cause under-delivery and inflate effective CPA.

**When to introduce Cost Cap on ASC:** After 90 days of data and ≥150 total purchases. Set cost cap at 20% above current average CPA, not at target CPA.

### Google Ads

| Campaign Type | Recommended Bid Strategy | Notes |
|--------------|--------------------------|-------|
| Brand Search | Target Impression Share (95%) | Own your brand SERP; low cost, essential |
| PMax — Core Products | Maximise Conversion Value (no ROAS target) | For first 30–60 days until 35+ conversions accrued |
| PMax — Scaled | Target ROAS once ≥50 conv/30 days | Start ROAS target at 20–30% below current actual ROAS to avoid under-delivery |
| Standard Shopping (if added) | Target ROAS | Gives more control than PMax for price-sensitive queries |

**Warning on Target ROAS:** Setting a ROAS target before Google has sufficient conversion data (≥35–50 events in 30 days) causes severe under-delivery. Many accounts set Target ROAS too early and conclude Google doesn't work, when in fact they prevented the algorithm from gathering the data it needed.

### TikTok Ads (When Budget Permits)

| Campaign Type | Recommended Bid Strategy | Notes |
|--------------|--------------------------|-------|
| Prospecting (Lowest Cost) | Lowest Cost | For volume; accept variable CPA during learning |
| Smart+ Campaign | Smart+ bidding (automated) | Use when budget reaches $1,500/mo on TikTok alone |
| Retargeting | Cost Cap at 1.5x target CPA | Only when retargeting pool exceeds 5,000 users |

**TikTok-specific note:** Cost Cap on TikTok works differently from Meta. TikTok's Cost Cap is a hard ceiling, not an average — it will under-deliver significantly if set too low. Set initial Cost Cap at 2x your target CPA during learning, then reduce as algorithm stabilises.

## 1.9 Kill / Scale Recommendations

### 3x Kill Rule Application

| Scenario | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| Any ad set spends 3x target CPA with zero conversions | Pause immediately | Budget is wasted on non-converting targeting or creative |
| TikTok ad group spends $150 with <2 conversions | Pause and consolidate budget to Meta | TikTok below minimum viable — redirect to stronger platform |
| Google PMax spends $500 in 30 days with <5 conversions | Review asset quality and feed, or switch to Standard Shopping | PMax may be misallocating to non-converting placements |
| Meta prospecting ad set: frequency >3.0 (7-day), CTR declining | Pause ad set, refresh creative | Creative fatigue — not a bidding problem |

### 20% Scale Rule

Before scaling any campaign budget: confirm the campaign has been stable for 7+ days with CPA at or below target. Never increase by more than 20% in a single adjustment. Wait 5–7 days between increases to allow the algorithm to re-stabilise.

### Priority Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation | Expected Impact |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Critical** | Consolidate to Meta Ads only (or Meta + Google Brand Search) | Doubles learning speed, exits Learning Limited status within 30–45 days |
| **Critical** | Pause TikTok paid campaigns until budget reaches $3,500+/month | Frees $300–$500 for productive spend on proven channels |
| **High** | Allocate 70% of total budget to Meta ASC | Meta ASC with £80+ AOV products and subscription LTV is highest-probability ROAS channel |
| **High** | Run Google Brand Search at $100–$150/mo regardless of other decisions | Protects brand SERP from Hello Klean and competitors at minimal cost |

## 2.2 UK GDPR & ICO Compliance Assessment

### Regulatory Context

The UK retained GDPR provisions post-Brexit under UK GDPR (Data Protection Act 2018 as amended). The ICO (Information Commissioner's Office) is the enforcement authority. Key distinctions from EU GDPR:

| Aspect | EU GDPR | UK GDPR | Implication for Curo Skin |
|--------|---------|---------|--------------------------|
| Consent standard | Explicit, granular | Same — explicit, granular | Cookie consent must be opt-in |
| Legitimate interest | Available (limited) | Available (limited) | Cannot rely on LI for marketing cookies |
| Enforcement | DPAs per country | ICO | UK fines issued to UK companies |
| Cross-border | EU SCCs required | UK IDTA/adequacy decisions | Meta/Google data flows require review |
| Consent Mode v2 | Mandatory for EU ads | Strongly recommended for UK | Functionally required for Google Ads |

### Critical Compliance Checks

| Check | Requirement | Status Assessment | Risk Level |
|-------|------------|-------------------|------------|
| Cookie consent banner present | Required | Unknown — must be verified | Critical |
| Banner is opt-IN (not pre-ticked) | UK GDPR Art 7 | Unknown | Critical |
| Granular consent categories shown | Analytics, marketing, functional separate | Unknown | High |
| Privacy policy covers ad tracking | Required | Unknown | High |
| Consent Mode v2 implemented | Google requirement + best practice | Likely absent (common gap) | Critical |
| Data retention limits applied | Required | Unknown | Medium |
| Consent records stored | Required | Unknown | Medium |

### ICO Enforcement Reality

The ICO has issued formal enforcement notices and fines to e-commerce businesses for non-compliant cookie consent. Specific risk areas for Curo Skin:

1. **Pre-ticked consent boxes** — automatically non-compliant, frequently seen on Shopify themes with default cookie apps
2. **Bundled consent** — "Accept all cookies" without granular marketing/analytics distinction is non-compliant under UK GDPR
3. **Dark patterns** — making "reject all" harder to find than "accept all" has been cited in ICO guidance as deceptive
4. **No consent, no cookies** — if a user does not consent, the Meta Pixel, Google tag, and TikTok Pixel must not fire. Many implementations still fire all tags regardless of consent state.

## 2.3 Consent Mode v2 Assessment

### What Consent Mode v2 Does

Google's Consent Mode v2 (mandatory for EU/EEA advertising since March 2024, strongly recommended for UK) allows Google's tags to adjust their behaviour based on user consent state. When a user does not consent to analytics or advertising cookies, Consent Mode v2 sends cookieless pings that Google uses to model conversions via machine learning.

Without Consent Mode v2, Google loses all conversion signal from non-consenting users. For UK audiences where consent rates are often 50–70%, this means losing signal from 30–50% of actual conversions.

| Consent Mode v2 Parameter | Purpose | Default if Not Set |
|--------------------------|---------|-------------------|
| `ad_storage` | Controls advertising cookies (Google Ads, Floodlight) | Denied — no conversion tracking |
| `analytics_storage` | Controls GA4 analytics cookies | Denied — no GA4 data |
| `ad_user_data` | Controls sending user data for ad personalisation | Denied |
| `ad_personalization` | Controls remarketing/personalised ads | Denied |

### Implementation Requirement

Consent Mode v2 must be implemented via Google Tag Manager (GTM) with a consent management platform (CMP) integration. The CMP must:
1. Collect consent before any tags fire
2. Pass consent state to GTM via the Data Layer
3. Update consent state if the user changes their preferences
4. Maintain a consent log for ICO audit purposes

**Recommended CMPs for Shopify UK:**
- Cookiebot (native GTM integration, UK GDPR compliant, ~£10–£20/mo)
- OneTrust (enterprise, ~£50–£100/mo)
- Consentik (Shopify App Store, free tier available)

**Priority: Critical** — Without this, Google Ads conversion tracking is potentially illegal and certainly unreliable for UK audiences.

## 2.4 Server-Side Tracking Assessment

### Why Server-Side Tracking is Non-Negotiable for UK DTC

Browser-based tracking (pixels, tags) is subject to:
- Browser ad blockers (estimated 25–40% UK usage)
- Safari ITP (Intelligent Tracking Prevention) — 7-day cookie expiry
- Firefox ETP — blocks known tracking domains
- User consent rejection — must not fire without consent
- Network latency and page abandonment before pixel loads

The combined effect is that browser-only tracking reports 30–60% of actual conversions, depending on the audience's technical sophistication and browser usage. Health/beauty/wellness audiences (Curo Skin's target) have above-average ad blocker usage.

### Meta Conversions API (CAPI)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| What it does | Sends conversion events directly from server to Meta, bypassing browser limitations |
| Setup on Shopify | Available via native Shopify-Meta integration (one-click CAPI in Shopify Admin > Channels > Facebook & Instagram) |
| Deduplication requirement | Both browser Pixel and CAPI events must include matching `event_id` to prevent double-counting |
| Data quality score | Meta reports Data Quality score (0–10) in Events Manager — target ≥7 |
| Test Events tool | Use Meta Events Manager Test Events to verify server events firing correctly |
| UK GDPR consideration | CAPI events must only be sent for users who have consented to marketing cookies |

**Assessment:** CAPI is almost certainly not fully implemented or properly deduplicated at this stage. Shopify's native CAPI integration is available but requires configuration, and deduplication (the `event_id` matching between browser and server) is consistently the most common failure point.

**Priority: Critical** — Without CAPI, Meta's algorithm receives incomplete signals and cannot optimise effectively. This is the single highest-leverage tracking fix available.

### Google Enhanced Conversions

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| What it does | Hashes first-party data (email, phone) at point of conversion and sends to Google to match against signed-in users |
| Setup method | Google Tag Manager or Google Ads UI (conversion action settings) |
| Data requirement | Requires order confirmation page to pass customer email/phone to GTM dataLayer |
| GDPR compliance | Must only activate for users who have consented to advertising cookies |
| Improvement | Typically recovers 10–30% of conversions lost to browser restrictions |
| Shopify implementation | Requires custom GTM dataLayer push on order confirmation page |

**Assessment:** Enhanced Conversions is likely absent. Setup requires developer assistance on Shopify to push hashed customer data to GTM. This is a Medium-priority item (after CAPI, which has greater impact).

### TikTok Events API

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| What it does | Server-side equivalent of TikTok Pixel — sends events directly from server |
| Shopify integration | Available via TikTok Shopify App (includes Events API option) |
| Deduplication | Requires matching `event_id` between Pixel and Events API — same principle as Meta CAPI |
| UK GDPR | Same consent requirement — only send for consented users |

**Assessment:** If TikTok Ads are running, Events API should be configured. However, given TikTok spend is likely minimal and the recommendation is to pause TikTok paid campaigns, this is a lower-priority item. Implement when TikTok paid activity resumes.

## 2.5 Event Deduplication Assessment

Deduplication is the most technically complex and most commonly broken element of server-side tracking setups. When both browser events (Pixel/Tag) and server events (CAPI/Enhanced Conversions/Events API) are running simultaneously, every conversion can be recorded twice — once by the browser and once by the server.

### How Deduplication Works

| Platform | Deduplication Mechanism | Common Failure Mode |
|----------|------------------------|---------------------|
| **Meta** | `event_id` parameter — must be identical in browser Pixel event and CAPI event | `event_id` not passed in browser event, or generated differently server-side |
| **Google** | `transaction_id` in Enhanced Conversions matches purchase events | Transaction ID not passed to GTM dataLayer correctly |
| **TikTok** | `event_id` (same principle as Meta) | TikTok App and custom Events API generating different IDs |

### Likely Deduplication Status

Given that:
1. CAPI is likely partially or incorrectly configured
2. Shopify's native CAPI integration handles deduplication automatically when used correctly, but custom implementations frequently miss this step
3. Google Enhanced Conversions is likely absent, so deduplication is not yet relevant

**Assessment:** Deduplication risk is HIGH if CAPI has been manually configured without following Shopify's native integration path. If using Shopify's built-in Facebook & Instagram sales channel for CAPI, deduplication is handled natively — but must be verified in Meta Events Manager (look for duplicate event rates in the Events Manager dashboard).

**Verification step required:** Check Meta Events Manager > Data Sources > your Pixel > Events > look for high "Deduplicated" percentage. If >15% of events are being deduplicated, the setup is working but the event_id generation may need review. If 0% are being deduplicated and both browser + server events are active, deduplication is broken.

## 2.6 Attribution Overlap & Cross-Platform Attribution Risk

### The Multi-Touch Attribution Problem at Low Budget

At $2,000/month across three platforms, the customer journey is complex relative to the conversion volume. A single purchase might be attributed to:
- Meta (last click before purchase)
- Google (last click in a separate browser session)
- TikTok (view-through attribution — TikTok claims 7-day view-through by default)

This means three platforms each report the same conversion, making ROAS figures from individual platforms meaningless without a cross-platform attribution tool.

| Attribution Risk | Severity | Impact |
|-----------------|----------|--------|
| TikTok view-through claims — 7-day window is aggressive | High | TikTok ROAS appears inflated; true contribution unclear |
| Google cross-network (if Audience Network enabled) | Medium | Low-quality placements inflate impression share |
| Meta view-through (1-day default) | Medium | Can be adjusted — reduce to click-only for cleaner data |
| All three platforms claiming same conversion | High | Blended MER becomes the only reliable performance indicator |

### Recommended Attribution Settings

| Platform | Recommended Setting | Why |
|----------|--------------------|----|
| **Meta** | Click-only attribution (no view-through) during learning phase | Cleaner signal; view-through inflates ROAS at low budget |
| **Google** | Last click for initial benchmarking, then Data-Driven once ≥300 conversions/month | Data-Driven requires sufficient volume; last-click is conservative baseline |
| **TikTok** | 7-day click, no view-through during testing | View-through attribution at low budget creates misleading efficiency data |

### The MER Solution

Because per-platform ROAS is unreliable when running multiple channels, MER (total revenue / total ad spend) is the correct primary KPI for Curo Skin at this budget level. MER captures the blended contribution of all channels without attribution distortion.

**Implementation:** Track total Shopify revenue weekly and divide by total ad spend across all platforms. This number is more actionable than any individual platform's reported ROAS.

### Post-Purchase Survey Recommendation

This is free to implement via Shopify Order Status page customisation or a Klaviyo post-purchase flow, and reveals dark social channels (word of mouth, organic social, PR) that no paid platform can track.

## 3.4 Creative Fatigue Risk Assessment

Creative fatigue is the primary performance risk at low budget. At $2,000/month across three platforms, the same small audience segments are being served the same ads repeatedly. Frequency builds fast.

### Fatigue Thresholds

| Platform | Fatigue Signal | Action Trigger |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Meta prospecting** | 7-day frequency >3.0 | Replace creative, expand audience, or reduce budget |
| **Meta retargeting** | 7-day frequency >8.0 | Audience pool too small — pause or reduce budget |
| **TikTok** | 7-day frequency >3.0 | Replace creative (TikTok fatigues faster than Meta) |
| **Google Display** | CTR declining >30% week-over-week | Refresh display creative set |

### Fatigue Risk at $2,000/month

At $2,000/month with a UK-focused shower filter audience (estimated addressable Meta audience: 1–3 million UK homeowners aged 25–55), fatigue will become apparent within:
- **Month 1–2:** Unlikely if creative set has 4+ assets
- **Month 3–4:** Likely if creative set has not been refreshed
- **Month 5+:** Certain if the same creatives are still running

### Creative Refresh Cadence (Recommended)

| Timeframe | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **Monthly** | Introduce 1–2 new creatives into Meta ASC; retire lowest-performing |
| **Every 6–8 weeks** | Full creative refresh on TikTok (when active) |
| **Quarterly** | Full creative strategy review; new angles, new hooks, new formats |
| **Event-triggered** | Immediately refresh if: frequency >3.0 (Meta) or CTR drops >25% week-over-week |

**Low-cost refresh strategy:** UGC is the most cost-effective creative refresh mechanism. A single creator brief sent to 3–5 micro-creators producing 1–2 videos each generates 5–10 new creatives for £500–£1,500. This is significantly more efficient than brand-produced video at comparable cost.

## 3.5 UGC Strategy Recommendations

Shower filters are an ideal UGC product. The usage scenario (shower routine) is universally relatable, the benefit is visually demonstrable, and the product is aesthetically designed for a bathroom setting. Yet UGC is consistently underprioritised by small DTC brands.

### Why UGC Outperforms Brand Creative for This Product

1. **Authenticity in a trust-sensitive category.** Skin and hair claims are subject to scepticism — a real person showing their results is more credible than a brand claim.
2. **Relatability.** "I tried this for 90 days" content performs because viewers see themselves in the creator.
3. **Algorithm preference.** Both TikTok and Meta reward content that holds attention. UGC storytelling (problem → solution → result) consistently outperforms polished product demos on attention metrics.
4. **Cost efficiency.** UGC costs £100–£400 per creator brief vs £2,000–£5,000 for brand-produced video.

### UGC Content Angles for Curo Skin

| Angle | Hook | Platform | Priority |
|-------|------|----------|---------|
| **Filter reveal after 90 days** | "I can't believe what came out of my shower water for 3 months" | TikTok, Reels | Critical |
| **Skin/hair transformation** | "My skin after switching to a filtered shower" | TikTok, Reels, Meta | Critical |
| **Shower routine GRWM** | "My GRWM shower routine ft. the product I can't live without" | TikTok | High |
| **Water quality test** | "I tested my tap water vs filtered shower water — [reaction]" | TikTok, YouTube | High |
| **Competitive comparison** | "Why I switched from Hello Klean / other brand" | TikTok | Medium |
| **Unboxing + first impression** | "First use of the Curo Skin shower filter" | TikTok, Reels | Medium |
| **Subscription normalisation** | "My quarterly filter delivery just arrived — here's why I love this" | TikTok, Reels | Medium |
| **Problem → solution** | "I've been struggling with dry skin / hair fall for years. Then..." | Meta, TikTok | High |

### UGC Creator Brief Requirements

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|---------|
| Creator profile | Micro-influencers (5K–50K followers), UK-based, wellness/beauty/lifestyle niche, real home environment |
| Platform format | TikTok-native 9:16 for TikTok; square or 9:16 for Meta Reels |
| Key messages | Must include: filter lifespan (90 days), skin/hair benefit, subscription convenience |
| Restrictions | No specific medical claims (skin condition cures) — reference "improved appearance" not medical outcomes |
| Hook requirement | Visual or verbal hook in first 1.5 seconds — brand name optional (can be added in post) |
| Rights | Request usage rights for paid amplification (Spark Ads / Meta whitelisting) at time of brief |

### Spark Ads Strategy (TikTok)

When TikTok paid activity resumes, prioritise Spark Ads (boosting organic posts from creator accounts) over standard in-feed ads. Spark Ads retain the creator's account handle and social engagement (likes, comments, shares), which dramatically increases CTR and reduces CPM compared to dark post in-feed ads.

**Spark Ads workflow:**
1. Send product to 3–5 UK micro-creators with UGC brief
2. Monitor organic performance for 5–7 days
3. Identify top-performing video (highest watch-through rate, engagement rate)
4. Request Spark Ads access code from creator
5. Boost via TikTok Ads Manager with targeted audience
6. Budget: $50–$100 to test; scale if CPA is acceptable

## 3.6 Before/After Content — Platform Restrictions

Before/after content is among the highest-converting creative formats for health and wellness products. However, platform policies restrict this format in ways that require careful navigation.

### Platform-Specific Restrictions

| Platform | Policy | Implication for Curo Skin |
|----------|--------|--------------------------|
| **Meta** | Prohibits before/after images that imply unexpected or unrealistic results; restricts ads depicting personal attributes (skin conditions, weight, etc.) | Skin before/after showing dramatic transformation may be rejected; water quality before/after is generally permitted |
| **TikTok** | Similar restrictions on health claims; "misleading" before/after prohibited; content making medical claims about skin conditions not permitted | Water quality / filter condition before/after is safer ground than skin claims; "my skin feels better" (subjective) vs "cures eczema" (prohibited) |
| **Google** | Display policy prohibits shocking or disturbing imagery; personalised advertising policies restrict health-related targeting | Less restrictive for non-medical product demonstration |

### Safe Before/After Angles

| Before/After Type | Platform Risk | Recommended Framing |
|------------------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Clean filter vs dirty used filter** | Low — no personal attribute involved | Show visually, let the image speak; add "90 days of filtered showers" overlay |

| **Hair texture / skin feel improvement** | Medium — subjective claim | Testimonial framing: "My hair feels softer" (personal experience, not medical claim) |
| **Hair loss reduction claims** | High | Avoid — health claim; use "I've noticed less hair in my drain" as subjective personal experience only |
| **Eczema / psoriasis improvement** | Very High | Do not use in paid ads — medical claim |

## 3.10 Creative Testing Framework

At low budget, creative testing must be structured to generate signal without wasting spend.

### Meta Creative Testing Protocol

| Phase | Method | Budget | Duration | Decision |
|-------|--------|--------|----------|----------|
| **Initial test** | Add 4–6 creatives to ASC; let algorithm allocate | Full ASC budget | 14 days | Identify top 2 performers |
| **Creative kill** | Remove creatives spending $20–$30 with no purchases | N/A | Ongoing | Preserve budget for winners |

| **New creative test** | Introduce 2 new creatives monthly into ASC alongside proven winners | Within existing budget | 14 days | Replace underperformers |
| **Angle test** | Test different hooks (social proof vs demo vs problem/solution) | Prospecting test budget | 14 days | Identify winning angle for next UGC brief |
